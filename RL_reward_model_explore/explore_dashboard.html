Reward-model exploration dashboard

Copy of RL_reward_model/ at RL_reward_model_explore/. Six iterations vs v2_improved_run: 1–3 architecture/capacity, 4–6 objective/checkpoint/IRM. Goal: better held-out pairwise accuracy (test fooled and not-fooled), gen-bias closer to the human 37% fool rate, usable score range. Original RL_reward_model/ source was not modified.

Current recommendation

Keep v2 as the production reward model. None of the six explore runs beat v2 on the joint goal (held-out test + fooled-test + not-fooled) without dropping fooled-all below 70%. Iters 4–6 did move the objective in the right direction: iter 4 hit the 37% human fool rate (prefers-gen 38%) and test 60.3%; iter 6 cut the train–test gap to 5.5pp with test 60.8%. Both paid for it in fooled detection.

If there is an iteration 7: keep the two-sided hinge, balanced checkpoint, and IRM. Add a hard fooled floor on checkpoint selection (reject snapshots with fooled-all < 70% or fooled-test < 60%) and search λ_cal in {2, 4} instead of 8. Do not add mixup or more encoder/FP capacity.

What we learned (after 6 iterations)

  • More capacity (large Morgan MLP) overfits Turing pairs in a few epochs. Freezing GINE does not stop memorization.
  • The v2 checkpoint composite (65% fooled + 35% overall) consistently selects the gen-biased snapshot.
  • A 37% generation prior works (iter 4 prefers-gen 38%) but λ_cal=8 recreates v1 conservatism. λ_cal=2 is closer to a usable tradeoff.
  • Two-sided ranking + balanced checkpointing is the highest-leverage change in this copy.
  • IRM-lite shrinks player leakage (train–test gap 16pp → 5.5pp) and lifts test to 60.8%, at a fooled cost.
  • Score range is easy (0.09 → 0.14–0.49) and not sufficient.
  • The remaining missing piece is a hard fooled floor on which snapshot gets saved — not more encoder/FP capacity, not mixup.
